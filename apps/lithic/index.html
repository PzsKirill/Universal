The problem

Digital memory is fragile.

Every format we use today needs power, upkeep and a migration every few years. Miss one, and the data is gone. Quartz needs nothing.

How it works

From your servers to the mountain, in four steps.

  1. Step 01 / 04

    Encode

    We turn your files into a self-checking pattern, with a picture guide at the start of every plate that explains how to read it.

    Any format · 3× error correction

  2. Step 02 / 04

    Etch

    A femtosecond laser writes the pattern inside fused quartz. The finished plate survives fire, water, magnets and time.

    360 TB per plate · 1.2 TB per hour

  3. Step 03 / 04

    Seal

    Plates travel in argon-filled cassettes to Vault 01, a chamber cut 1,200 metres into the mountain, and are sealed in.

    −4 °C · no power · no network

  4. Step 04 / 04

    Retrieve

    Ask for a copy and get it in 48 hours. Or, a thousand years from now, read the plate with any optical microscope.

    48 h retrieval · no LITHIC required

FAQ

Good questions.

The things people ask before they trust a mountain with their records.

Ask your own →
01 What happens if LITHIC closes down?

Custody passes to an independent trust funded at signing. And every plate carries its own reading instructions, so no LITHIC software is ever needed to read it.

02 How do I get my data back?

Request a retrieval and we return a certified copy within 48 hours. In the far future, an optical microscope and the picture guide etched on every plate are enough.

03 Is it secure?

You can encrypt data before it is etched. The vault has no network connection at all, and opening it takes three custodians and two physical keys.

04 Can data be changed or deleted?

No. Etching is permanent, which is the point. For collections that keep growing, we etch new plates on a schedule.

05 How much fits on one plate?

Up to 360 TB on a 120 × 120 mm plate. Family tiles are smaller and hold up to 100 GB.

06 Why a mountain in Norway?

Bedrock that has been stable for 1.8 billion years, a constant −4 °C, low seismic risk and a strong rule of law.
